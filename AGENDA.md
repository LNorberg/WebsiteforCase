## 2. The brief

Willow Tree Publishing is a fictional, 40-year-old traditional publisher, comparable to Scholastic. It built its name on illustrated children's picture books and memorable characters, then expanded into newspapers, fashion magazines, novels, cookbooks, and other print. It has a strong reputation and a loyal, multi-generational customer base, but sales are declining and growth is slowing as consumers move to digital platforms, short-form content, and other entertainment, especially younger audiences.

Willow Tree is not entirely offline: it runs a corporate website and active social accounts, but these are promotional and distribution channels rather than meaningful revenue sources. Most revenue still comes from physical publications.

**Objective:** design a strategic growth and revitalisation plan that reverses declining sales, identifies new growth opportunities, and establishes a sustainable competitive advantage. The organisers explicitly welcome digital expansion, leveraging existing IP, new business models, or other directions.

**Questions the deck must answer:**

1. Market and consumer trends: which shifts create the biggest challenges and opportunities?
2. Growth opportunities: which products, services, markets, or business models should Willow Tree pursue?
3. Brand positioning and competitive advantage: how should it use its strengths, IP, and reputation to differentiate?
4. Financial and operational feasibility: what investments, operational changes, and financial considerations does the plan require?

## 4. Market context (evidence base)

Primary source: IBISWorld, *Book Publishing in the US* (industry 51113), February 2026, unless noted.

- **Flat industry.** US book publishing revenue is estimated at $48.9B in 2026, growing at only 0.6% a year over 2021–26 (0.3% in 2026 itself), with a forecast of 0.4% a year to $49.8B by 2031. Profit margin is about 12.7%. The industry cannot absorb many cost increases.
- **Children's books are the resilient print category.** Children's books are about $4.9B (9.9% of industry revenue). In Q1 2026 they were the only print category with positive unit sales growth, suggesting parent and educator spending is holding up.
- **But institutional buyers are squeezed.** School library budgets are falling about 4.3% a year, and more than half of libraries report unmet materials needs. The K-12 student population is a key demand driver and is shrinking in places.
- **Audio is the growth format.** US audiobook sales reached $2.22B in 2024 (+13%, Audio Publishers Association). Digital audio now makes up 18.4% of adult trade revenue, overtaking e-books (15.4%) for the first time. AI narration accounts for about 23% of new audiobook releases and is growing quickly, which cuts per-title production cost.
- **Franchises win.** Successful children's properties (Dog Man, Diary of a Wimpy Kid) are extended into film, gaming, and licensed merchandise. Securing audio, translation, and adaptation rights is increasingly critical.
- **Hits concentrate demand.** Social discovery (BookTok) pushes sales toward a small number of viral titles and authors, while sometimes reviving deep backlist. Author-led marketing matters more.
- **Subscriptions are growing.** Kindle Unlimited, Scribd, Audible Plus, and Spotify Audiobooks+ normalise recurring access; publishers are partnering with them for recurring revenue.
- **Cost pressure.** Paper prices and the 2025–26 tariff regime raise costs, with China-printed illustrated children's books most exposed. Booksellers project retail prices could rise 5–10%.
- **Competitor benchmark.** Scholastic monetises book fairs and school channels and owns significant IP.

**Figures from team notes still needing a source:** US children's book market of $3.3B and global children's publishing market of $10.76B; category split of US book sales (adult fiction 42%, children's and YA 33%, adult non-fiction 25%); children's sub-format split (paperback 45%, hardcover 20%, interactive/digital 20%, board books 15%). **Open:** the $3.3B figure conflicts with IBISWorld's $4.9B; pick one source and cite it consistently on the slides.

## 5. Working recommendation (draft)

The emerging thesis: Willow Tree's most valuable, under-used asset is its library of beloved characters and the trust it holds with parents and grandparents. The plan turns that IP into recurring revenue streams, builds a real digital presence, and trims the parts of the portfolio that drag.

### 5.1 Stories and IP (primary pillar)

- **License, don't sell, the characters** to TV, film, and animation partners so Willow Tree earns royalties on external productions without bearing production cost. Main focus of the TV strategy.
- Target public and educational broadcasters (a PBS-style partner) that fit the brand's family trust.
- Use short-form content as a low-cost pilot for longer TV concepts (the notes cite Jellycat-style character content as inspiration).
- Develop new characters and storylines, including reaching new demographics and under-represented communities (accepting this may not be profitable at first).
- Capitalise on nostalgia: market to Gen X and boomer parents and grandparents who grew up with the characters.

### 5.2 Online presence and marketing

- Split social presence into sector brands: Willow Tree Kids (read-alouds, short-form tests, character hosts), Willow Tree Lifestyle (cookbooks, host or face), Willow Tree Fashion.
- A branded YouTube channel positioned around "healthy screen time" with read-aloud videos.
- Author-led marketing: advance reader copies to reviewers and influencers, authors narrating their own audiobooks, authors breaking down their novels, cooks and models presenting lifestyle titles. Possibly a Masterclass-style format with authors.
- A specialised digital marketing branch with a team per sector.
- Audiobooks, with AI-driven narration as a cost lever for backlist titles.

### 5.3 Recurring revenue models

- **Book subscription box:** monthly box, bundles of adult and children's books, for predictable revenue.
- **Library partnerships:** subscription discounts or a partnership with a national library system or organisation; monthly themed displays; characters on library cards.
- **Membership and licensing** are both framed as recurring, more predictable revenue.

### 5.4 Distribution and new markets

- School channel: book fairs and school-district partnerships (positioned against Scholastic), including a writing competition where the winning story becomes a featured short on YouTube.
- Online storefront (direct to consumer) and bulk wholesale direct to schools.
- Community organisations (Girl Scouts, 4-H, Big Brothers Big Sisters, Rotary) for advertising and retail.
- International: English-language content is widely consumed abroad, and children learning English (e.g. Sweden) are a growth market.
- Education market: reselling used books.

### 5.5 Portfolio rationalisation and funding

- Cut or sell underperforming lines, starting with newspapers.
- License underperforming magazine titles for a lump sum to reinvest in the growth pillars.
- **Open:** whether to mention asset-backed financing on future royalties (the notes reference "Bowie bonds") as a funding mechanism.

### 5.6 Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Digital cannibalises print | Digital is a discovery tool, not full access to the catalogue |
| Licensing dilutes the brand | Choose partners carefully; issue content guidelines |
| Content doesn't convert to sales | Run small pilots before scaling |
